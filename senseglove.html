Senseglove

Turning glove calibration into a personalised entry point into VR

Senseglove 2024 UX Design Haptics / VR
Year 2024
Role UX Design Intern
Ownership Research, narrative design & 3D flow prototype

(01) — Context

Context

This was an internship during my Master's in Design for Interaction at TU Delft, for Senseglove, a haptics company. We wanted to create a solution that helps users calibrate their haptic gloves using haptics and visuals — the moment right after putting on a VR headset, when the user can't see their real surroundings and has to sync their real hands with the virtual ones on screen before the experience can begin.

To understand the starting point, I mapped out the existing calibration scenario: a user wearing VR glasses sees a menu, a vanishing point marking the ground plane, shadow hands already opening and closing to demonstrate the motion, their own VR hands replicating their movements, and instruction text asking them to open and close their hands (and not forget their thumb) until the virtual hands begin to move.

(02) — Approach

Approach

I ran user research and product analysis around this existing flow, working closely with 3D visuals and haptics. I built a persona — an autonomous first-time user, like someone on a client's marketing team trying the kit for the first time, expecting it to be easy, intuitive, and futuristic — and mapped their full journey action by action: what they see at each step, how they feel, and where the experience could improve. The emotional curve told the story clearly: curiosity and excitement at the start, dipping into confusion, information overload, and frustration through the middle of calibration, before only recovering once calibration ended and the real experience began.

That mapping surfaced the core gap: there was a lack of a relatable narrative that could immerse people in such a foreign scenario. So the design goal became to make calibration feel like a personalised entry point into the VR experience — a seamless transition toward complete immersion — guided by five criteria: intuitive calibration that anyone can follow autonomously, a real sense of body ownership over the virtual hands, clear step-by-step guidance, a pace that balances the real and virtual worlds, and easy troubleshooting when calibration isn't going as expected.

01

Body Ownership

The first thing users see should make them start believing the VR hands are their own.

02

Clear, Guided Steps

Users are walked through calibration step by step, with troubleshooting built in if something goes wrong.

03

Seamless Transition

Completion of calibration flows directly and gently into entry into the VR experience.

(03) — Outcome

A portal into VR

The outcome was a narrative where users transition into the VR experience from the real world like stepping through a portal — calibration became the story, not an obstacle before it. I tested the concept with users through low-fidelity prototypes, then built out the full flow in 3D: the environment, the moment hands materialise, the pacing of each step, and the visual and color language of the experience.
