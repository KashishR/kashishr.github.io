Parking Pal

A people-friendly bike parking experience at Delft Station

TU Delft 2023 UX Design Physical Interaction
Year 2023
Role UX Designer & Researcher
Ownership Research through final prototype

(01) — Context

Context

Delft Station's bike parking facility holds 5,000 bicycles. Finding your bike after parking it is stressful and confusing, especially for commuters in a hurry to catch a train. This was my master's project at TU Delft, where I worked as UX Designer & Researcher across the full process, from research to a working prototype.

(02) — Approach

Approach

I began with contextual research: observing user behavior during peak hours, shadowing commuters through their full parking and retrieval journey, and mapping emotional responses to different scenarios using PreMo and emotion typology. This grounded a clear design goal — to help people in a hurry locate their bike effortlessly after parking it. From there, I ran Ultra Rapid Prototyping and co-creation sessions with commuters to explore ways to help people remember and relocate their spot, revealing that memory aids combined with physical wayfinding elements showed the most promise.

(03) — Outcome

A two-part system

The result was a two-part system. The final prototype brought it to life across three components: a detailed 2D layout of the full user flow, a 3D printed miniature model of the physical installation, and a working prototype with functional lighting and display elements for user testing.

Part 1

While Parking

A sentence lights up when the user parks — they read and remember it as their location marker.

Part 2

While Finding

Words on the ground guide users back to their original sentence through intuitive wayfinding.
